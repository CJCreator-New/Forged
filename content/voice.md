# Voice

## Tone
- Clear, direct, curious. Explains a big idea in plain terms, then asks the reader a question.
- Analytical but not academic. Shows the pattern, then names the strategic question.

## Audience
- Peers, hiring managers, product and people leaders. Assumed to know the basics.

## Sentence rules
- Average sentence length: short, mostly 6 to 14 words. One-line paragraphs are common.
- Contractions: yes ("I've", "isn't", "It's", "isn't simply").
- Opening style: a bold claim or reframe in one line, such as "X is no longer just Y." or "The real question is...".

## Use these
- "The real question is", "The bigger question", "The interesting part", "The strategic", "It is becoming"
- Arrow chains to show a pattern, such as "Prompt → Response" then "Goal → Context → Tools → Execution → Deliverable"
- Lists of named items on their own lines, with no bullets, when describing a platform's parts
- Ending on a question or a one-line implication

## Never use
- Emojis
- Em dashes (the long dash)
- Engagement bait ("comment YES", "tag someone who...", "like if you agree")
- Stock AI words: leverage, delve, game-changer, unlock, in today's fast-paced world, here's the thing

## Samples
- "AI is no longer just a competition between models. The real competition is becoming ecosystem vs ecosystem."
- "Claude's story is increasingly becoming a story about knowledge work and software engineering. The strategic shift is fascinating: Prompt → Response is becoming Goal → Context → Tools → Execution → Deliverable."
- "DeepSeek matters beyond the chatbot itself. It sits at the intersection of frontier intelligence, open deployment and inference economics."
